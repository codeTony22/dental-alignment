# The Opportunity — Growth Forecast & Scenarios

*A forward look at what the portal and automation could unlock. Figures are an illustrative model built on a representative starting point of ~200 implant units/month at ~$50/unit — substitute your real numbers; the multipliers (margin, capacity, growth) hold regardless of the absolute baseline. Estimates, not guarantees.*

---

## 1. The shift in one sentence

Today your growth is capped by hours — every case is hand-built, and admin eats time you could spend on cases. The portal removes the admin ceiling and lets you add shops without adding staff; the automation removes the *labor* ceiling, so the same team produces far more, at a far lower cost per case. Growth stops being a function of how many hours you can work.

## 2. Three reinforcing levers

- **Margin expands** — automation strips the mechanical, non-clinical labor out of each case, so every unit costs less to produce. Cement-retained cases, which skip the hardest step entirely, approach near-marginal cost.
- **Capacity expands** — by automating the prep and registration work, one operator handles roughly twice the cases per day. The bottleneck moves from "hours available" to "shops to serve."
- **Volume expands** — a professional self-serve portal plus faster, more reliable turnaround is a sales asset: it wins new shops and keeps them, and it scales to shops in new geographies (e.g. WhatsApp-heavy markets) without a local presence.

The three compound: more cases, each more profitable, produced by the same team.

## 3. Unit economics — today vs after automation

| Per implant unit | Today (manual) | After automation | Cement-retained (best case) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price to shop | ~$50 | ~$50 | ~$50 |
| Cost to produce | ~$17 | ~$9 | ~$6–7 |
| **Gross margin** | **~$33 (66%)** | **~$41 (82%)** | **~$43 (86%)** |

The price doesn't have to change for profitability to jump — the cost side does the work. And because cement-retained crowns are the most automatable case type, they become your highest-margin, lowest-effort volume.

## 4. Capacity unlock

A skilled operator hand-builds on the order of ~15 implant units a day. Automating the file prep, mesh cleanup, and implant-position recovery roughly **doubles that to ~28–30 a day** — the same person, no new hire, ~2× the output. That headroom is what lets volume grow without payroll growing in step.

## 5. Two-year scenarios

Monthly run-rate, from the illustrative ~200 units/month start. (These are the curves in the trajectory chart.)

| | Today | End of year 1 | End of year 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Conservative** — portal only, modest growth | 200 u/mo | 230 | 265 |
| **Base** — portal + automation | 200 | 270 | 365 |
| **Optimistic** — + aggressive shop growth, cement-retained wedge, rush tier | 200 | 320 | 510 |

Translating to annualized run-rate at the end of year 2:

| Scenario | Year-2 revenue (run-rate) | Year-2 gross profit | vs today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Today | ~$120k | ~$79k | — |
| Conservative | ~$159k | ~$105k | **1.3×** |
| Base | ~$219k | ~$180k | **2.3×** |
| Optimistic | ~$306k | ~$254k | **3.2×** |

The gap between conservative and optimistic is almost entirely the margin jump (66%→82%) plus the capacity to say yes to more shops — both of which the automation provides.

## 6. What this unlocks beyond the numbers

- **Turnaround as a competitive weapon.** Automation-assisted speed enables a same-day or next-day tier — which both wins shops from slower competitors and justifies premium pricing on rush work.
- **A near-zero-marginal-cost product line.** Once cement-retained crowns run through the automated path, each one costs almost nothing in labor — a segment you can price aggressively to capture volume, or keep at high margin.
- **Geographic reach without offices.** The portal plus WhatsApp notifications let you serve dental shops in markets where you have no physical presence.
- **A data moat that compounds.** Every case you process feeds the system's learning loop, steadily widening the share of cases that run automatically — your cost-per-case keeps falling as your case history grows, a flywheel competitors starting from zero can't match.
- **Optional second business.** The same platform that runs your lab could, down the line, be offered to *other* labs — turning an internal tool into a product line.
- **Premium tiers.** Rush, white-label for larger groups, and volume contracts all become easy to offer once the operational backbone is in place.

## 7. The build pays for itself

In the base scenario, the extra gross profit by year 2 is on the order of **~$100k/year above today** — which recovers the full portal-plus-automation build (roughly $65–130k) inside about a year, while the portal-plus-spike stage (~$30–50k) pays back in a matter of months on admin savings and capacity alone. The optimistic path recovers it considerably faster. Importantly, the stages are gated, so spend follows proven results — you are never committing the full amount on faith.

## 8. Honest assumptions (so the numbers are believable)

- The baseline (~200 units/month) is illustrative — **your real current volume is the number to plug in**; the percentages and multiples are what carry over.
- The margin and capacity gains assume your caseload skews toward the automatable case types (simple and cement-retained crowns). A caseload dominated by complex full-arch work automates less, and those gains would be smaller.
- Volume growth depends on your sales and shop relationships — the software *enables* the growth, it doesn't guarantee it.
- Automation earns back its build cost only above a moderate monthly volume; below that, the portal alone is the win and automation waits.

The point of the range is honesty: even the conservative path is a healthier, more scalable business, and the realistic upside — same team, far more output, much better margins — is genuinely large.
